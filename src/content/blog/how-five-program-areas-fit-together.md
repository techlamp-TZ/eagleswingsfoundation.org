Eagle's Wings has five program areas: neuro-diversity awareness, elderly care support, education and therapy guidance, life skills, and caregiver training. They are designed to fit together, because a person's needs, their family and their community are all connected. This guide shows, step by step, how to find the area that is closest to your question.

The information is educational. It is not medical, legal or clinical advice. Please speak with a qualified professional about diagnosis, treatment or medication.

## Step 1: Start with the person and the question

Before looking at any list, describe what you hope to understand or improve. It might be a question about how a child communicates. It might be worry about an older relative's memory, a wish to help a young person become more independent, or a need for your own support as a caregiver.

Writing down the question in a sentence helps you pick the right doorway. Keep the person's strengths in mind too, not only the difficulties.

## Step 2: Meet the five program areas

Here is a quick overview.

1. **[Neuro-diversity awareness](/programs/program-neurodiversity)** covers Autism, ADHD, Down syndrome and intellectual disability. It focuses on understanding, respect and reducing stigma.
2. **[Elderly care support](/programs/program-elderly-care)** offers guidance on nutrition, physical exercise and mental stimulation for older people, including those living with Alzheimer's and dementia.
3. **[Education and therapy guidance](/programs/program-education-therapy)** explains neurological conditions, therapy exercises and specialised parenting in plain language.
4. **[Life skills programs](/programs/program-life-skills)** give structured life-skills support for children with autism, especially young people 12 and above.
5. **[Caregiver training](/programs/program-caregiver-training)** helps caregivers build confidence and look after their own wellbeing.

## Step 3: See where they overlap

The areas are not separate boxes. Here are some generic examples, offered only as illustrations.

> **Good to know:** These are imagined examples, not real people. Imagine a family with an autistic teenager. They might read about autism in the awareness area, practise cooking steps in the life-skills area, and join caregiver training to share worries with others.
>
> Imagine an adult child caring for a parent living with dementia. They might use elderly care guidance for nutrition and gentle movement, and caregiver training for their own rest and stress.

The WHO notes that [more than 55 million people worldwide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dementia) live with dementia, and that [about 1 in 100 children](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/autism-spectrum-disorders) has autism. These figures show how many families may be looking for understanding, and why connected guidance can be useful.

## Step 4: Choose one small next step

You do not need to master everything at once. Pick one action:

- Read the page for the program area closest to your question.
- Write down two questions to bring to a doctor or therapist.
- Try one small change at home, such as a simple daily routine.
- Contact us through our [contact page](/contact) if you have a question about the Foundation.

Physical activity of any kind should be supervised and suited to the person. Water activities in particular need close supervision.

## Step 5: Involve professionals where it matters

Our program areas offer education and guidance, not diagnosis or treatment. If you are worried about a child's development, an older person's memory or a change in health, please see a qualified professional. We would rather you ask early than wait.

## Why do we start focused?

It might seem that a foundation should offer everything at once. We take a different view. Eagle's Wings was founded in 2025, and we believe that a young organisation earns trust by doing a limited number of things carefully.

Starting focused means:

- **Quality before quantity.** Each area is prepared properly before we stretch further.
- **Safety first.** Activities such as swimming and exercise need trained supervision.
- **Promises we can keep.** We say what we do, and we do not claim what we cannot show.
- **Room to listen.** We can adapt to what families and caregivers tell us.

We explain this thinking further in [how Eagle's Wings approaches responsible program growth](/blog/article-responsible-program-growth).

## Where the areas meet our vision

Each area supports one or more of the four pillars in our vision: community inclusion, rights and dignity, empowerment and independence, and early support. Awareness supports inclusion and dignity. Life skills support independence. Education, therapy guidance and caregiver training support early support. See [the four pillars in daily practice](/blog/article-four-pillars-of-our-vision-in-daily-practice) for a closer look.

## What this means at Eagle's Wings

The five program areas are five doors into one house. You can enter through whichever one matches your question, and you will find the others connected inside. Browse them all on our [programs](/programs) page.
