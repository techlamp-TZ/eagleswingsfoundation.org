Our mission is to empower, encourage and educate less privileged people, people with special needs and older people by helping, caring for and supporting their needs. Our vision is a community where people with disabilities are valued equally and get the support they need to reach their full potential. This article explains both statements in everyday words.

Eagle's Wings Foundation Tanzania is a non-profit founded in 2025 and based in Tegeta, Dar es Salaam. We put our purpose in writing so that anyone can hold us to it.

## What is the difference between a mission and a vision?

Think of a journey. The mission is what we do on the road each day. The vision is the place we hope to reach.

| | Mission | Vision |
|---|---|---|
| Question it answers | What do we do, and for whom? | What kind of world do we hope for? |
| Time frame | Now and every day | The future we work towards |
| Ours in short | Empower, encourage and educate | Full inclusion and equal value |

Both matter. A mission without a vision can become a list of tasks. A vision without a mission is only a wish.

## The mission, word by word

### Empower

To empower is to help a person gain confidence, skills and choice over their own life. It is different from doing things for someone. In practice, this can be as simple as patiently teaching a household task, or explaining a therapy exercise so a family feels able to try it.

### Encourage

Encouragement means noticing what a person can do and building on it. It avoids pity. A child, a young person or an older person who is encouraged is more likely to try again.

### Educate

Education runs in two directions. We share clear information with families, caregivers and communities. We also learn from them. The information we offer is general and educational. It never replaces advice from a doctor, therapist, nurse or social worker.

### Helping, caring for and supporting needs

These three phrases describe practical support. They are about listening to what a person actually needs, rather than deciding on their behalf.

## Who is "less privileged, special needs and older"?

The mission names three groups of people: those who are less privileged, people with special needs, and older people. Our work focuses on people with disabilities and challenges, especially children and older people, including those living with Alzheimer's and dementia. We also support their families and caregivers.

We use the phrase "special needs" because it appears in our founding statement. In our daily language we prefer to say "person with a disability", "autistic person" or "person with autism" (both are widely used), and "person living with dementia". We describe support needs, not weaknesses.

## The vision in plain words

Our vision is full inclusive awareness. That means people are aware, in the everyday sense, that people with disabilities belong in community life and are valued equally. It also means they receive comprehensive support, meaning help that looks at the whole person and family rather than one part of life.

The phrase "reach their full potential" is easy to misread. We do not use it to mean "become like everyone else". We mean that each person should have the chance to grow in their own way, at their own pace.

> **Good to know:** The vision matches an idea found in the UN [Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html), adopted in 2006 and in force since 2008. It is built on dignity, autonomy, non-discrimination, participation and inclusion, accessibility and respect for difference.

## How the vision becomes four practical pillars

Our vision rests on four pillars: community inclusion; rights and dignity; empowerment and independence; and early support. Each turns a big idea into something a person can recognise in daily life. We describe them one by one in [our article on the four pillars](/blog/article-four-pillars-of-our-vision-in-daily-practice).

## Why we write these promises down

The WHO estimates that [about 1.3 billion people, around 16% of the world's population,](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/disability-and-health) experience significant disability. Behind that number are individual people and families, many of whom feel unseen. Naming our purpose openly keeps us honest. If our actions drift from our words, people can and should point it out.

It is also why we talk about values and approach. We do not claim results we cannot prove. We explain that approach in [how Eagle's Wings approaches responsible program growth](/blog/article-responsible-program-growth).

## What this means at Eagle's Wings

Our mission is what we try to do each day, and our vision is what we hope our community can become. You can read both in full on the [mission, vision and values](/about#mission) page, and see who we are on the [About page](/about).
