A short checklist can turn a stressful appointment into a useful conversation. Before you go, gather your records, write down what you have noticed, list your questions and prepare your child gently. This article gives a step-by-step checklist you can adapt for a therapy visit or an assessment.

## Why does preparation help?

Appointments can feel rushed and emotional. You may be worried, tired or travelling a long way through Dar es Salaam traffic. In that state it is easy to forget the very thing you meant to say.

Preparation does not mean being perfect. It means giving the professional a clear picture of your child, and giving yourself a calm way to take part. You know your child better than anyone, and your observations are valuable.

## The checklist before the day

### Gather what you already have

- Earlier reports from doctors, therapists or teachers, if any exist
- Health records and a list of any medicines your child takes (bring the packaging or the doctor's note, and never change medicines without medical advice)
- School reports or teacher comments
- Your child's name, date of birth and any identification the service asks for
- Contact details for other people involved in your child's care

### Write down what you have noticed

Spend a few days jotting simple notes. Focus on what you actually see and hear, not on labels.

- What is your child good at, and what do they enjoy?
- What do they find hard, and when does it happen?
- How do they communicate what they want or need?
- How do they sleep, eat and move through the day?
- What helps when they are upset or overwhelmed?
- What worries you most right now?

Starting with strengths is important. An assessment is not only about difficulties. It should describe the whole child.

### Write your questions

Questions are easy to forget under pressure. Write them on paper and put the most important ones first. Some ideas are below.

## Questions worth asking

**About the visit itself**

- What will happen today, and how long will it take?
- Can I stay with my child throughout?
- Will I receive a written summary?

**About what you hear**

- Can you explain that in simple words?
- What are my child's strengths, as you see them?
- What does this mean for daily life at home and at school?

**About next steps**

- What should we try first, and how will I know if it is helping?
- Can you show me how to do the exercise or activity?
- When should we come back, and who should I contact with questions in between?
- Are there other professionals we should see?

> **Practical tip:** Bring a second adult if you can. One person can listen and ask questions while the other writes notes. If that is not possible, ask permission to write during the conversation.

## Preparing your child

How you prepare your child depends on their age, communication and needs. A few gentle ideas:

1. **Explain in simple, honest words.** For example: "We are going to meet someone who helps children learn and play. You will not be in trouble."
2. **Describe the steps.** Tell them where you will go, who they may meet and what may happen.
3. **Use pictures or a simple schedule** if your child understands routines better that way.
4. **Pack comfort items.** A favourite toy, a snack that is allowed, a drink of water and a quiet activity can help.
5. **Plan the day around rest.** Try not to schedule other demanding activities right before or after.

If your child finds new places difficult, ask whether a short, calm visit beforehand is possible.

## During the appointment

- Ask the professional to slow down whenever needed.
- Say if something does not match what you see at home. Your insight matters.
- Speak about your child with respect, as they may be listening.
- Ask for any recommendation to be written down.
- Before you leave, repeat the plan in your own words to check you understood.

If you feel overwhelmed, it is fine to say so and ask for a short pause.

## After the appointment

Take a few minutes while it is fresh.

- Write down the main points and the next steps.
- Choose one small action to begin this week.
- Share the plan, in simple form, with other adults who care for your child, such as grandparents or a teacher.
- Give your child a kind word or a small treat that suits them. Attending is hard work for them too.
- Look after yourself. It is common to feel relief, sadness, worry or all of them together. Talking to someone you trust helps.

## What a checklist cannot do

This checklist is general education. It cannot diagnose or decide what your child needs. Only qualified professionals can carry out an assessment and recommend therapy. If you are concerned about your child's development or health, please seek a qualified assessment as soon as you can, since early support is one of the ideas at the heart of good care.
