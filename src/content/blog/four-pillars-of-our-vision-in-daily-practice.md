The Eagle's Wings vision rests on four pillars: community inclusion, rights and dignity, empowerment and independence, and early support. Each pillar is only useful if it shows up in ordinary days. This checklist turns them into questions you can ask yourself at home, in a classroom or in the neighbourhood.

It is a reflection tool, not an audit and not a scorecard. You do not need to tick every box. Use it to notice what is already going well and where to start.

## Pillar one: is this person included in community life?

Community inclusion means that people with disabilities are active and contributing members of their community, not isolated from it. In Tanzania, where extended family and neighbours are so important, this can be very practical.

Ask yourself:

- [ ] Is the person invited to family gatherings, celebrations and community activities?
- [ ] Are there roles the person can take on, such as helping with a meal, greeting guests or looking after a plant?
- [ ] Can the person reach the places where others gather, such as the home, school, place of worship or market?
- [ ] Do the people around them talk *with* them, not only *about* them?
- [ ] Are neighbours and relatives given honest, respectful information, so misunderstanding does not lead to avoidance?

## Pillar two: are rights and dignity respected?

Rights and dignity mean that every person is treated as equal in value and has a voice in decisions about their life. The UN [Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html), adopted in 2006 and in force since 2008, is built on dignity, autonomy, non-discrimination, participation and inclusion, accessibility and respect for difference.

Ask yourself:

- [ ] Do we ask the person what they want before deciding for them?
- [ ] Do we use respectful words, such as "person with a disability", and avoid pity or labels?
- [ ] Do we protect privacy, including personal care and health information?
- [ ] Do we ask permission before sharing a photo or a story?
- [ ] If someone is treated unfairly, is there a trusted adult they can tell?

> **Practical tip:** A simple habit is to pause before helping and ask, "Would you like some help with that?" Then wait for the answer.

## Pillar three: is independence growing?

Empowerment and independence include vocational training, independent-living skills and income. The aim is not to do everything alone. The aim is more choice and more skill over time.

Ask yourself:

- [ ] Is the person learning one small daily-living skill at a time, such as washing, tidying or preparing food, with patient practice?
- [ ] Are tasks broken into steps and shown, not only explained?
- [ ] For older youth and adults, are there chances to learn a trade or a useful skill?
- [ ] Does the person have some say over how they spend their time and, where possible, their money?
- [ ] Do we celebrate effort, not only perfect results?

The ILO recognises that people with disabilities have the right to decent work. Preparing for that starts long before adulthood, with confidence built in small everyday tasks. Our program on [life skills](/programs/program-life-skills) explores this idea for autistic young people.

## Pillar four: is support early enough?

Early support includes early intervention therapies, family counselling and caregiver education. Getting help early is often easier than waiting, and it helps the whole family.

Ask yourself:

- [ ] If a child seems to be developing differently, have we sought an assessment from a qualified professional, such as a doctor or therapist?
- [ ] Do parents and caregivers have a place to talk about worries and feelings?
- [ ] Are caregivers learning how to support the person, rather than being left to guess?
- [ ] Do we know the trusted health and social-care contacts in our area?

Please note that only qualified professionals can diagnose, recommend therapy or advise on medication. Nothing here replaces that.

## How the four pillars work together

The pillars support one another. A child who receives early support is more likely to gain skills. A person with more skills can take part more fully. A community that includes people is more likely to respect their rights. And when dignity is respected, people feel safe enough to try new things.

| Pillar | One everyday question |
|---|---|
| Community inclusion | Who is missing from this gathering, and why? |
| Rights and dignity | Did we ask the person first? |
| Empowerment and independence | What is one small step towards doing this together? |
| Early support | Who could we ask for guidance today? |

## A note about honesty

We share these pillars as our approach and hope. We do not claim that they have produced particular results. They are the standards by which we hope to be judged.
