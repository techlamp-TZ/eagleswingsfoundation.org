Healthy boundaries, strict confidentiality and everyday respect keep children and older people safe and keep volunteers on solid ground. Use the checklist below before, during and after every volunteering session. It is general guidance. Your organisation's own safeguarding rules always take priority.

## Why do boundaries matter?

Children and older people, especially those with disabilities or who live with dementia, may depend on others for daily support. That dependence can create an imbalance of power, even when everyone means well. Boundaries are the agreed limits that keep the relationship safe, clear and respectful.

Boundaries are not coldness. You can be warm, patient and cheerful while still keeping a clear role.

## Checklist 1: Personal boundaries

Tick each one before a session.

- [ ] I know my role and what tasks I have agreed to do.
- [ ] I will not do personal care, lifting or exercise support unless I am trained and assigned to it.
- [ ] I will not be alone with a child or vulnerable person in a private space unless the organisation's rules clearly allow it.
- [ ] I will not give gifts, money or favours outside the organisation's arrangements.
- [ ] I will not make private arrangements with families or share my personal contact details unless the organisation agrees.
- [ ] I will not give medical advice or suggest changes to medication or treatment.

## Checklist 2: Confidentiality

What you see and hear is not yours to share.

- [ ] I treat names, health information, family situations and behaviour as private.
- [ ] I do not discuss people I support in public places, with friends or on social media.
- [ ] I ask before taking any photo or video, and I follow the organisation's rules on what may be shared.
- [ ] I store notes safely and do not leave documents where others can read them.
- [ ] I share concerns only with the responsible coordinator.

> **Good to know:** Confidentiality has a limit. If someone may be at risk of harm, you must pass the concern on to the responsible person. Never promise a child or older person that you will keep a worrying disclosure secret.

## Checklist 3: Respectful language and behaviour

Respect is shown in small choices.

- [ ] I speak to the person directly, at their eye level, in a calm tone.
- [ ] I use person-respectful words such as "person with a disability", "autistic person" or "person with autism", and "person living with dementia".
- [ ] I ask before I help and I offer choices.
- [ ] I avoid talking about someone in front of them as if they were not there.
- [ ] I do not use baby talk with adults, even when they are older or living with dementia.
- [ ] I avoid pity, praise that sounds surprised ("You did that so well!") and labels that reduce a person to a condition.

## Checklist 4: Safety in activities

- [ ] I follow supervision instructions at all times.
- [ ] I do not lead water, swimming or exercise activities unless I am assigned and trained.
- [ ] I know who to call in an emergency.
- [ ] I tell a coordinator if someone seems unwell, distressed or unsafe.

The World Health Organization notes that children and older people can face a greater risk of violence and neglect, which is one reason clear safeguards matter. Volunteers are part of the protective circle around them.

## Comparing healthy and unhealthy patterns

| Healthy pattern | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| Warm, consistent, clear role | Special favourites or secret friendships |
| Asking permission before helping | Taking over without asking |
| Sharing concerns with the coordinator | Keeping worries to yourself |
| Following photo and sharing rules | Posting about people online |
| Admitting what you do not know | Giving advice beyond your training |

## Checklist 5: After the session

- [ ] I report any incident, injury or concern the same day.
- [ ] I leave personal information about people at the venue or in safe storage.
- [ ] I reflect on what went well and what to ask about next time.
- [ ] I look after my own wellbeing and talk with a coordinator if a session was upsetting.

## What if I am not sure?

Ask. No responsible organisation will criticise a volunteer for checking. Asking protects the person you support and protects you.

The UN [Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html) is built on dignity, autonomy, non-discrimination and respect for difference. Each item on this list is a small way of living those principles.
