Most myths about autism, ADHD, Down syndrome and intellectual disability come from fear and lack of information. The facts are simpler and kinder: these are natural differences in human development, they are common, and they are not anyone's fault. Here are answers to questions families and communities often ask.

## Is autism rare?

**Myth:** Autism is rare, so I will never meet an autistic person.

**Fact:** The World Health Organization estimates that [about 1 in 100 children worldwide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/autism-spectrum-disorders) has autism. It is a wide spectrum, and support needs vary greatly. If you go to school, work, worship or shop, you have almost certainly met autistic people, perhaps without knowing it.

Many are also adults. Autism does not end at childhood.

## Is it caused by bad parenting or a curse?

**Myth:** A child is autistic, has ADHD or has Down syndrome because of something the mother did, or because of witchcraft or a curse.

**Fact:** No. Parenting style does not cause autism or ADHD. Down syndrome is caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21, which happens by chance. Blame hurts families and stops them from seeking help. Mothers, fathers and caregivers deserve support, not judgement.

> **Good to know:** If you hear blame or fear spoken about a child, you can gently say, "This is a difference, not a punishment, and the family needs our support."

## Are people with these conditions unable to learn or work?

**Myth:** A person with a disability cannot learn, work or live a full life.

**Fact:** Ability varies widely, and many people learn, work and build independence with the right support and opportunities. Some need help with daily tasks, and others need very little. The WHO estimates that [about 1.3 billion people, around 16% of the world's population,](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/disability-and-health) experience significant disability. They are a huge part of humanity and of every workforce, school and community.

Assuming a person cannot do something is often the biggest barrier of all.

## Can ADHD be fixed by discipline?

**Myth:** A child with ADHD is simply naughty or lazy and needs stricter discipline.

**Fact:** ADHD is a difference in how the brain manages attention, activity and impulses. Harsh punishment does not cure it and can harm a child's confidence. Structure, clear routines, movement breaks and encouragement help many children. Decisions about treatment, including any medication, should be made with a doctor.

## Is there a cure for autism or Down syndrome?

**Myth:** With the right treatment or prayer, autism or Down syndrome will go away.

**Fact:** Autism and Down syndrome are lifelong. This is not a reason for despair. Therapy, education, health care and inclusion can help a person develop skills and enjoy life. Be careful of anyone who promises a miracle cure or asks for large payments. Speak to a qualified health professional before trying any treatment.

## Do autistic people not have feelings?

**Myth:** Autistic people do not care about others or do not feel emotions.

**Fact:** Autistic people have feelings, friendships and love, just like everyone else. They may express them differently, for example by sharing a favourite interest, helping with a task or being loyal and honest. Different expression is not missing emotion.

## Should people with disabilities be kept at home?

**Myth:** It is better to protect a child or older person with a disability by keeping them at home.

**Fact:** Isolation harms wellbeing and limits opportunity. The UN [Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html), adopted in 2006, is built on dignity, participation and inclusion. Safe, supported participation in school, play, worship and community life helps people belong. Protection and inclusion can go together with planning and supervision.

## Myths and facts at a glance

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Autism is rare | About 1 in 100 children has autism, per WHO |
| Bad parenting causes it | Parenting does not cause autism or ADHD |
| People with disabilities cannot contribute | With support, many learn, work and contribute |
| ADHD is just bad behaviour | It is a difference in attention and impulse control |
| A cure will make it disappear | These are lifelong; support improves quality of life |
| Autistic people lack feelings | They feel deeply and may express it differently |

## How can I help change these myths?

1. Share accurate information with family and friends.
2. Use respectful words, and correct harmful ones kindly.
3. Invite people with disabilities to take part, and listen to them.
4. Support families instead of judging them.
5. Ask qualified professionals when you are unsure.

This article is general education and not medical advice.
