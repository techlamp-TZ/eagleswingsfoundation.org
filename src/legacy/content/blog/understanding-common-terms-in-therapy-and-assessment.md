Many words used in therapy and education are simple ideas in technical clothing. This guide explains common terms in plain language so you can follow appointments with confidence. It is general education only, and a qualified professional should explain what any term means for your own child.

## Why does the language feel so hard?

Professionals learn specialised words during training and use them daily. Families hear them for the first time, often at a stressful moment. You do not have to learn all of them. It is always fine to say, "Please explain that in simpler words."

The terms below are grouped by theme. Meanings can vary slightly between services and countries, so treat them as a starting point.

## Words about finding out what a person needs

### What is an assessment?

An assessment is a process of gathering information about a person's abilities, needs and daily life. It may include talking with the family, observing the person, playing or working on tasks together, and reading reports. A good assessment describes strengths as well as challenges.

### What is a diagnosis?

A diagnosis is a name that a qualified professional may give after an assessment, such as autism or Down syndrome. It can help families find support and understand what to expect. A diagnosis does not define a person's worth or limit what they can become.

### What does screening mean?

Screening is a quick check to see whether a fuller assessment may be useful. It is not a diagnosis. If a screening raises questions, a qualified professional can explain the next step.

## Words about support

### What is therapy?

Therapy means planned support to help a person build skills, manage challenges and take part in daily life. There are different kinds, for example:

- **Speech and language therapy:** communication, understanding and, in some cases, feeding and swallowing.
- **Occupational therapy:** everyday skills such as dressing, eating, play and handwriting.
- **Physiotherapy (physical therapy):** movement, strength and balance.
- **Behavioural or psychological support:** understanding feelings, behaviour and coping.

Which kind is suitable depends on the person, and a qualified professional should decide.

### What is early intervention?

Early intervention means support offered early in a child's life, when they are learning and growing quickly. It can include therapy, play-based learning and guidance for the family. The goal is to help a child reach their potential and to give families tools and confidence. Support is valuable at any age, so do not feel it is too late.

### What is a therapy plan or goals?

A therapy plan is a set of steps agreed between the professional and the family. Goals are the small, specific things the plan aims for, such as "asks for help using a word or gesture". Good goals are realistic and meaningful to the family.

### What is specialised parenting guidance?

This is support that helps parents and carers understand a child's needs and learn practical ways to encourage learning, communication and independence at home. It does not mean parents did anything wrong.

## Words about differences in how people learn and think

### What is neuro-diversity?

Neuro-diversity is the idea that people's brains work in a range of ways, and that this variation is natural. The term is often used for autism, ADHD and other differences in learning and thinking. It encourages us to support people rather than try to make everyone the same.

### What is autism?

Autism is a lifelong difference in how a person communicates, interacts and experiences the world. It is described as a spectrum because it looks different for every person and support needs vary greatly. The World Health Organization estimates that [about 1 in 100 children worldwide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/autism-spectrum-disorders) has autism. Some people prefer "autistic person" and others "person with autism", so it is good to ask what someone prefers.

### What is ADHD?

ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder) is a condition linked to differences in attention, activity levels and impulse control. Support can include understanding, routines, school adjustments and, where advised by a doctor, other treatments.

### What is intellectual disability?

Intellectual disability means a person finds it harder to learn, reason and manage everyday skills, and needs support with these. It begins in childhood. People with intellectual disability have strengths, personalities and dreams like anyone else, and with the right support many take part fully in community life.

### What causes Down syndrome?

Down syndrome is caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21. It is a lifelong condition, and people with Down syndrome learn and grow in different ways and at their own pace, and can be part of school, work and family life.

## Words about school and rights

### What is inclusive education?

Inclusive education means children with different abilities learn together, with support and adjustments so that everyone can take part. It is based on respect and belonging.

### What are reasonable adjustments, or accommodations?

These are practical changes that help a person take part, such as extra time, a quiet space, pictures to explain a task or a different way of showing what they know.

### What is the CRPD?

The UN [Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html) was adopted in 2006 and came into force in 2008. It is built on dignity, autonomy, non-discrimination, participation, inclusion, accessibility and respect for difference.

> **Practical tip:** Keep a small list of new words from each appointment, with the meaning as explained by the professional. Over time you will build your own family glossary.
