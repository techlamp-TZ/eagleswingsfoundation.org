If you would like to volunteer with Eagle's Wings Foundation, the place to start is the volunteer interest form on the [Get Involved](/get-involved) page. The questions below cover general good practice for new volunteers. For anything specific to the Foundation, such as roles, schedules or requirements, please ask the team directly rather than assuming.

## Getting started

### How do I begin?

Fill in the volunteer interest form on the Get Involved page. You can also read the [volunteers page](/volunteers) for an overview. If you have questions first, use the details on the [contact](/contact) page.

### What information should I be ready to share?

Think about why you want to volunteer, the skills you could offer and how much time you realistically have. Being honest at this stage helps everyone plan well.

### Is there anything I should not assume?

Yes. Do not assume particular roles, schedules or entry requirements. Those are decisions for the organisation, and the team is the right source for accurate answers.

## Understanding the work

### Who does the Foundation support?

Eagle's Wings Foundation works to improve the lives of people with disabilities and challenges, especially children and older people, including those living with Alzheimer's and dementia, and to support their families and caregivers. You can see the five [program areas](/programs) for more detail.

### Why does this work matter?

The World Health Organization estimates that [1.3 billion people, about 16% of the world's population,](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/disability-and-health) experience significant disability. It also reports that [more than 55 million people worldwide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dementia) live with dementia. Families and caregivers often provide much of the daily support, so extra help from the community can make a real difference when it is offered carefully.

### Are volunteers replacing professionals?

No. Volunteers add time, company and practical help. Diagnosis, therapy and medical decisions stay with qualified professionals. A good volunteer understands and respects that line.

## Preparing yourself

### What attitude should I bring?

- Patience. People move and communicate at different paces.
- Curiosity. Learn what each person prefers instead of guessing.
- Humility. You will learn from staff, families and the people you support.
- Respect. Speak to the person directly and ask before helping.

### How should I talk about disability and dementia?

Use respectful, person-first language such as "person with a disability" or "person living with dementia". Some autistic people prefer "autistic person" and others prefer "person with autism", so it is fine to ask which they like. Avoid words such as "suffers from" or "handicapped", and avoid pity or "miracle" language.

> **Practical tip:** Write down your questions before a first visit. Good questions about safety, privacy and expectations show that you take the role seriously.

### What about privacy?

Treat everything you see and hear about people and families as confidential. Do not take or share photos, names or stories without clear permission from the organisation and the person or their family.

## Practical concerns

### How much time should I offer?

Offer what you can keep up. Predictable, steady help supports people who value routine. You can always increase your commitment later.

### What if I am not sure I am the right person?

That is a normal feeling. Talk it through with the team. It is far better to ask than to stay silent and drop out later, which can leave people without help they expected.

### Can I volunteer with my professional skills?

Often yes, when the organisation agrees the scope in advance. Teaching, writing, design and organisation are examples of skills volunteers may share. Clinical treatment and medical advice are different, because they carry professional responsibilities. The UN Volunteers site is a helpful general resource about how volunteering works around the world.

## A short readiness list

1. I have read about the Foundation's work and values.
2. I know how much time I can honestly give.
3. I understand that I will follow guidance and supervision.
4. I will respect privacy and use dignified language.
5. I am ready to ask when I am unsure.

## What this means at Eagle's Wings

Eagle's Wings Foundation welcomes people who want to give time responsibly. We ask you to begin with the volunteer interest form on the [Get Involved](/get-involved) page, and to read more on the [volunteers page](/volunteers). For general questions about the Foundation, see the [FAQ](/faq) or use the [contact](/contact) page.
