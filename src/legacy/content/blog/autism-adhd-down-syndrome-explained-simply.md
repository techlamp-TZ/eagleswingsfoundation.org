Autism and ADHD are natural differences in how the brain develops and works. Down syndrome is a genetic condition caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21. All three are lifelong, all vary widely from person to person, and all are best understood through a person's strengths as well as their support needs.

## Why explain these together?

Families and teachers often meet these words at the same time, and they can feel confusing. This article gives a simple starting point. It is general education, not medical advice, and it cannot replace an assessment by a qualified professional.

Notice that we say "a person with autism" or "an autistic person". Both are used. It is respectful to ask what someone prefers.

## What is autism?

Autism is a difference in how a person communicates, interacts with others and experiences the world. The World Health Organization describes it as a wide spectrum, and estimates that [about 1 in 100 children worldwide](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/autism-spectrum-disorders) has autism. Support needs vary greatly. Some autistic people need little daily support, while others need a lot.

Common signs can include:

- Preferring routine and finding change stressful.
- Being very sensitive, or less sensitive, to sound, light, touch or taste.
- Communicating in different ways, such as fewer words, gestures, pictures or devices.
- Deep interest in particular topics.

These are not flaws. Many autistic people bring honesty, focus and creativity to their families and communities.

## What is ADHD?

ADHD stands for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder. The CDC describes it as a condition that can make it harder to pay attention, control impulses or manage high levels of energy. It often begins in childhood and can continue into adult life.

A child with ADHD may:

- Find it hard to stay with a task that is not exciting.
- Move a lot, or feel restless when sitting for long.
- Act before thinking, or interrupt often.
- Be very energetic, curious and quick to notice new things.

ADHD is not caused by poor parenting or laziness. Structure, movement breaks, clear instructions and encouragement often help. Any decision about treatment or medication belongs with a doctor.

## What is Down syndrome?

Down syndrome happens when a person is born with an extra copy of chromosome 21. It is not caused by anything a parent did, and it is not contagious. People with Down syndrome usually have some degree of learning difference, and may also have particular health needs, so regular health care is important.

Alongside this, people with Down syndrome are often warm, sociable and determined. With early support, education and inclusion, many learn new skills, build friendships and take part in daily life and work.

## How do they compare?

| | Autism | ADHD | Down syndrome |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | A difference in communication, social interaction and sensory experience | A difference in attention, activity level and impulse control | A genetic condition from an extra copy of chromosome 21 |
| Range | Wide spectrum | Varies by person and setting | Varies by person |
| Often helpful | Predictable routines, clear communication, sensory comfort | Structure, movement breaks, short clear tasks | Early support, patient teaching, regular health care |
| Assessed by | Qualified professionals | Qualified professionals | Health professionals |

This table is a simplification. Real people do not fit neatly into columns.

## What helps, whatever the label?

Some support ideas work across all three:

1. **Learn the person, not only the label.** Ask what they enjoy and what is hard for them.
2. **Use clear, simple words.** Show as well as tell, using gestures or pictures.
3. **Keep routines predictable,** and prepare people for change.
4. **Celebrate effort and small steps.**
5. **Include them.** Games, chores, meals and classes are all chances to belong.
6. **Care for caregivers.** Rest and community support matter.

> **Practical tip:** If you are worried about a child's development, write down what you notice, such as how they play, speak or react to sounds. Bring these notes to a doctor or specialist. It makes the conversation easier.

## Are these conditions only a challenge?

No. Challenges are real, and families deserve support. But the strengths-based view reminds us that difference and ability live together. The [1.3 billion people, about 16% of the world's population,](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/disability-and-health) whom WHO estimates experience significant disability are not a separate group. They are part of every community.
